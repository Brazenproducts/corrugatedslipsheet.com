Slip Sheet Surface Friction and Load Stability Explained

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • October 2026

The Paradox of a Good Slip Sheet

A slip sheet must do two opposite things well. It has to slide easily onto and off the push-pull platen, yet it must grip the product above it firmly enough that the load does not shift during transport or handling. This paradox is governed almost entirely by coefficient of friction (COF)—a specification that buyers frequently overlook and vendors rarely publish clearly. Understanding how COF shapes load stability is essential to specifying a sheet that is both easy to handle and safe in transit.

Two Surfaces, Two Different Jobs

A well-engineered slip sheet is not uniform. The bottom surface, which contacts the platen and the warehouse floor during the pull, should have a low COF so the sheet glides smoothly under the gripper's pull force. The top surface, which supports the product, benefits from a higher COF so cases resist sliding when the load is accelerated, braked, or cornered. Some premium sheets are engineered with differential surfaces or coatings to optimize each face independently. When evaluating a sheet, ask specifically about top-face and bottom-face COF values—a single average figure hides the detail that actually matters.

How COF Interacts with Load Pattern

Friction is only one variable in load stability; it works together with the stacking pattern and any securing method. A tightly interlocked column stack with high top-face friction may need no additional stretch wrap, while a loosely patterned load will shift regardless of COF. The practical approach is to test your actual product cases on the candidate sheet surface, measuring the angle at which cases begin to slide. This slide-angle test is a simple, repeatable proxy for whether the top-face COF suits your products and handling speeds.

Material and Texture Choices

PP corrugated board offers consistent, engineerable surface properties that fiber slip sheets cannot match batch to batch. Elipacko's PP corrugated slip sheets can be produced with textured or treated top surfaces to raise product-side friction while keeping the bottom face smooth for clean pulls. Because the resin and texture are controlled in manufacturing, COF stays consistent across every sheet in a lot—critical for operations that cannot tolerate variable load behavior.

Environmental Factors That Change COF

Surface friction is not static across conditions. Cold-chain environments, condensation, dust, and residue all alter effective COF, sometimes dramatically. A sheet that holds cases securely in a dry ambient warehouse may become slick with frost in a freezer. For cold-chain and wet applications, specify a surface validated under your actual temperature and humidity conditions, and re-run the slide-angle test in the real environment rather than relying on lab figures.

  • Bottom face: low COF for smooth platen pull and reduced gripper load.
  • Top face: higher COF matched to case material and handling speed.
  • Environment: validate COF at actual temperature and moisture.
  • Consistency: demand lot-to-lot repeatability, a PP advantage over fiber.

Durability and Cost Over the Program Life

COF performance must hold up across the reusable life of the sheet. Textured top surfaces can wear smooth over many trips, gradually reducing load retention. Ask the vendor for grip-retention data after repeated wash and handling cycles, and build a periodic COF spot-check into your quality program. A durable sheet that maintains its friction profile across 50-plus trips protects both load safety and the cost-per-trip economics that justify reusable slip sheets over disposable fiber.
