Corrugated Slip Sheet Load Ratings and Gauge Guide

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • October 2026

Specifying Slip Sheets by the Numbers

Choosing a corrugated slip sheet is a specification exercise. The sheet must carry the full unit-load weight during push-pull handling without tearing, delaminating, or sagging. Procurement teams that specify gauge, lip design, and dimensions precisely get sheets that perform reliably and last for many cycles; those who order by generic description often end up with sheets that fail early. This reference covers the core parameters that drive slip-sheet performance.

Material and Flute Profile

Polypropylene corrugated, or twin-wall PP, is the dominant reusable slip-sheet material because it combines strength, moisture resistance, and washability. The sheet is built from two flat faces bonded to vertical flutes that give it rigidity. Flute direction matters: orienting flutes along the pull direction improves tensile strength during push-pull handling, while cross-fluted designs balance stiffness in both axes. For heavy or dense loads, higher-density flute spacing increases load capacity.

Gauge and Wall Thickness

Gauge, expressed in millimeters of overall sheet thickness or in grammage (grams per square meter), is the primary strength driver. Common slip-sheet gauges range from roughly 2 mm for light loads up to 5 mm or more for heavy industrial applications.

  • Light loads under 500 kg: thinner gauge, lower grammage
  • Medium loads 500 to 1,000 kg: mid-range gauge with reinforced lips
  • Heavy loads above 1,000 kg: thick gauge, dense flute spacing
  • Cold or wet environments: sealed edges and moisture-resistant resin

Elipacko supplies PP corrugated slip sheets in a range of gauges and grammages, manufactured from FDA-compliant resin, so buyers can match thickness to actual load weight rather than over- or under-specifying.

Lip Design and Dimensions

The lip is the tab the push-pull attachment grips. A single-lip sheet suits most push-pull operations, while two- or four-lip designs add support and allow handling from multiple sides. Lip length must match your attachment's gripping jaw, typically 50 to 100 mm. Overall sheet dimensions should match the load footprint with minimal overhang, since excess material wastes cost and can snag during handling, while too little coverage leaves product unsupported.

Load Rating Verification

A published load rating reflects performance under defined conditions. When comparing sheets, confirm whether the rating refers to static load, dynamic handling load, or tensile pull strength, because these differ significantly. For critical applications, request pull-strength data and run a loaded pilot before scaling. A sheet rated for your maximum gross load with a safety margin of 20 to 30 percent will deliver the cycle life that makes reusable sheets economical.

Standard sizes rarely fit every load profile. Custom dimensioning eliminates overhang, matches lip count to your equipment, and tunes gauge to the load.
