Cost-Per-Trip Economics of PP Corrugated Slip Sheets

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • October 2026

Slip sheets replace the pallet with a thin, high-strength sheet that a push-pull attachment handles in place of forks. The appeal is obvious on the loading dock, but the number that wins budget approval is cost-per-trip: the landed cost of a sheet divided by the cycles it survives. For logistics teams, modeling that figure correctly reframes the entire pallet-versus-sheet decision.

What Slip Sheets Remove From the Cost Stack

A wood pallet adds weight, cube, and recurring repair or disposal cost. On export lanes it also triggers ISPM-15 treatment and inspection. A slip sheet weighing a fraction of a pallet eliminates that overhead, frees container height for more product, and sidesteps fumigation entirely. Those structural savings exist before you even compare acquisition price, and they compound on every trip.

Building the Model

A credible cost-per-trip model for slip sheets includes acquisition cost, cycle life, return-logistics cost where the loop is closed, and shrinkage. Single-use kraft or fiber sheets complete one trip and absorb moisture, so their per-trip cost is simply unit price times every shipment. Reusable PP corrugated sheets carry a higher unit cost but are engineered for many cycles, and they do not degrade when wet, which is where the per-trip math turns decisively in their favor.

Consider a lane moving 1,500 loads weekly. Single-use sheets at modest unit cost still generate relentless recurring spend with zero residual value. A reusable PP fleet amortized across dozens of trips typically crosses below that cumulative line within the first operating year on repeatable lanes.

Sourcing to Protect the Model

The reusable case depends on controlling acquisition cost. Buying from a direct manufacturer strips distributor markup, and Elipacko supplies PP corrugated slip sheets made in Thailand and Vietnam at 0% antidumping duty, insulating the per-trip figure from tariff swings that quietly inflate other imports. The resin is FDA-compliant, which matters when sheets double as tier or layer separators in food and pharma loads.

Cold-Chain and Moisture Performance

Single-use sheets fail fastest in refrigerated and frozen environments, where condensation and humidity destroy fiber strength. PP corrugated sheets are non-absorbent and dimensionally stable across the cold-chain temperature band, so they hold load integrity trip after trip in exactly the conditions that defeat paper. That stability extends realized cycle life, and every extra cycle lowers cost-per-trip without a dollar of additional spend.

Right-Sizing the Spec

Cost-per-trip improves when the sheet is specified correctly: the right gauge and lip configuration for the push-pull attachment and load weight. Over-spec wastes material; under-spec shortens life through tearing and curl. Elipacko offers custom corrugated plastic sheets cut to exact dimensions, gauge, and lip count, which both reduces waste and extends service life, feeding directly back into lower per-trip cost.

Accounting for the Return Trip

Open-loop lanes complicate reuse, but PP sheets stack flat and weigh almost nothing, so return freight per unit is minimal when the loop can be closed. Honest models assume some shrinkage; the counterweight is durability that routinely beats conservative cycle assumptions.

Making the Call

Run the model with real lane volumes, test the cycle-life and shrinkage assumptions, and compare 24-month cumulative spend rather than a single purchase order. For most repeatable logistics lanes, reusable PP corrugated slip sheets win on cost-per-trip while also cutting weight, cube, and export overhead. The discipline is refusing to let unit price stand in for total cost of ownership.
