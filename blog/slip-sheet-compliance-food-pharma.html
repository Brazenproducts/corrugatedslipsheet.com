Slip Sheet Compliance for Food and Pharmaceutical Operations

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • September 2026

Why Compliance Matters for Pallet Layer Materials

In food processing and pharmaceutical manufacturing, every material that contacts or supports a product — including pallet layer pads, tier sheets, and push-pull slip sheets — is subject to regulatory scrutiny. FDA inspectors, third-party food safety auditors (SQF, BRC, FSSC 22000), and pharmaceutical GMP compliance teams increasingly require documentation that packaging and handling materials are food-safe, cleanable, and free from contamination risk.

Corrugated slip sheets, often overlooked in packaging compliance reviews, are a legitimate audit focus area. Wood pallets and corrugated cardboard slip sheets present documented contamination risks — splinters, moisture absorption, mold growth, and pest harborage — that PP corrugated alternatives eliminate.

FDA 21 CFR Compliance for Slip Sheet Materials

For slip sheets that will contact food products directly — or be used in environments where airborne contamination from the sheet could reach open product — the resin must comply with FDA 21 CFR 177.1520, which governs polypropylene resins for food contact use.

Not all PP corrugated is produced with food-contact-grade resin. Industrial-grade PP corrugated may include UV stabilizers, colorants, or processing aids that are not approved for food contact. When specifying slip sheets for food applications, explicitly request FDA 21 CFR 177.1520-compliant resin and obtain written certification from the manufacturer.

FSMA Preventive Controls and Packaging Materials

The FDA's FSMA Preventive Controls for Human Food rule requires facilities to identify and control hazards that could affect food safety, including hazards introduced through packaging and handling materials. For a food plant using slip sheets in direct proximity to open product or on lines where sheet fragments could enter the product stream, the material specification becomes a food safety preventive control.

PP corrugated slip sheets support FSMA compliance in several ways:

  • Non-porous surface: PP corrugated does not absorb moisture, which eliminates the mold and bacterial growth risk associated with wet cardboard or wood.
  • Cleanability: PP corrugated surfaces can be sanitized with food-grade cleaning agents between uses, supporting a reusable slip sheet program with documented wash cycles.
  • No splinters or fiber contamination: Unlike wood pallets and cardboard, PP corrugated does not shed fibers, splinters, or particulates that could contaminate product.
  • Pest resistance: PP corrugated provides no food source or harborage for rodents or insects, a key consideration for facilities managing integrated pest management (IPM) programs.

GMP Requirements in Pharmaceutical Pallet Handling

In pharmaceutical manufacturing and distribution, GMP guidelines — whether FDA cGMP (21 CFR Parts 210/211) or EU GMP Annex 15 — require that materials used in storage and handling do not introduce contamination risk to finished or intermediate product.

Wood pallets are explicitly flagged in many pharmaceutical facility SOPs as a contamination risk due to moisture, mold, and pest harborage. PP corrugated slip sheets used on top of wood pallets, or as full pallet base sheets in lieu of wood pallets, help facilities create a documented barrier between wood surfaces and pharmaceutical packaging.

For pharmaceutical warehouse and logistics operations, custom corrugated plastic sheets can be specified with heat-sealed edges (to eliminate open flute channels that could harbor debris), FDA-compliant resin, and color coding by zone or product line to support traceability requirements.

Third-Party Audit Readiness

Food safety certification bodies — including SQF, BRC Global Standards, and FSSC 22000 — audit the materials used in food handling environments as part of their facility assessments. Auditors may request:

  • Material safety data sheets (MSDS) for slip sheets and layer pads
  • Resin certification letters confirming food-contact compliance
  • Cleaning and sanitization protocols for reusable slip sheets
  • Evidence that retired or damaged sheets are removed from service

Building an audit-ready file for your slip sheet specification requires documentation from the manufacturer, not just a purchase order. Ensure your supplier can provide resin certifications, MSDS, and — for custom configurations — dimensional drawings and test reports.

Designing a Compliant Reusable Slip Sheet Program

For food and pharmaceutical operations transitioning to reusable PP corrugated slip sheets, a compliant program includes:

  1. Written specification: Document caliper, size, resin grade, edge treatment, and FDA compliance requirement.
  2. Supplier certification file: Maintain resin certifications and MSDS from the manufacturer on file.
  3. Wash SOP: Define cleaning agent, concentration, temperature, contact time, and rinse protocol.
  4. Inspection and retirement criteria: Define what damage (cracks, delamination, embedded contamination) triggers retirement from the food-contact environment.
  5. Trip count tracking: Log trips per sheet batch for life-cycle management and cost documentation.

A well-documented reusable slip sheet program is not just a cost-reduction initiative — it's a demonstrable food safety improvement that auditors respond to positively. The shift from disposable cardboard or wood slip sheets to cleanable, food-safe PP corrugated is increasingly viewed as standard practice in high-care food manufacturing environments.
