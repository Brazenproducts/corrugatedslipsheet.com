Slip Sheet Sizing Guide: Lip Length, Overhang, and Underhang Specs for Clean Handling

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • November 2026

Getting Slip Sheet Dimensions Right the First Time

A slip sheet only performs well when its dimensions match the load footprint and the push-pull attachment on your forklift. Order it too small and the load overhangs the sheet, losing support at the edges. Order it too large and the sheet underhangs past the load, wasting material and creating a trip hazard on the warehouse floor. This spec reference walks through the core dimensional decisions so your first order ships to the right size.

Understanding the Lip

The lip is the extended tab that the push-pull attachment grips to pull the load onto forks and push it back onto the floor or trailer. Lip length is the single most important dimension to specify correctly, because the attachment has to be able to clamp it cleanly.

  • Single-lip sheets: One gripping tab, used when loads are only handled from one direction. Typical lip length runs 2 to 4 inches.
  • Multi-lip sheets: Two, three, or four lips for loads handled from multiple sides or transferred between operations. Each lip follows the same length guidance.
  • Lip clearance: Confirm the lip length against your specific push-pull model, since gripper jaws vary by manufacturer.

Load Footprint, Overhang, and Underhang

The body of the slip sheet, excluding the lip, should match the base footprint of your unit load. Two tolerances govern that match:

  • Overhang: When the load extends past the sheet edge. A small, controlled overhang of up to about half an inch is acceptable, but excessive overhang removes edge support and risks product damage.
  • Underhang: When the sheet extends past the load edge. Keep underhang minimal, generally under three-quarters of an inch, so sheets do not catch on racking, interleave awkwardly in stacks, or protrude in trailers.

The practical target is a sheet whose body matches the load footprint within those tolerances, plus the lip allowance on the gripped side or sides. Measure the actual load, not the pallet it was built on, since slip-sheeted loads sit directly on the sheet.

Gauge and Wall Thickness Selection

Corrugated PP slip sheets are specified by wall thickness and areal density. Thicker, denser sheets carry heavier loads and survive more cycles, while lighter sheets reduce cost for lighter or single-trip applications.

  • Light duty: Thinner twin-wall PP for loads under roughly 1,500 lb and shorter reuse cycles.
  • Medium duty: Mid-weight sheets for general palletized freight in the 1,500 to 2,500 lb range.
  • Heavy duty: Higher-density, thicker-wall sheets for loads above 2,500 lb and demanding closed-loop reuse.

Match gauge to both the static load and the number of trips you expect, since reuse count drives cost-per-trip more than unit price alone.

Coefficient of Friction Considerations

Sizing is only half the stability equation. The sheet surface also needs enough friction to keep the load from sliding during transit while still releasing cleanly from the push-pull. Textured or treated PP surfaces balance grip and release. When you specify dimensions, note your load weight and stacking plan so the supplier can recommend a surface finish alongside the gauge.

Specifying a Custom Size

Standard slip sheet sizes rarely match every load exactly, which is why cut-to-size ordering matters. Provide the load footprint length and width, the number and position of lips, the lip length for your attachment, and the target load weight and trip count.
