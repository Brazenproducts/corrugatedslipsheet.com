Slip Sheets and Freight Density: How Eliminating Pallets Cuts Shipping Cost

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • November 2026

For shippers paying by the container, trailer, or cubic foot, the single largest hidden cost in palletized freight is the pallet itself. A standard wood pallet consumes roughly 5 to 6 inches of vertical load height and adds 40 to 70 pounds of dead weight per unit load. Corrugated slip sheets reclaim nearly all of that space and weight. This analysis quantifies the freight-density gains and shows procurement and logistics teams how to build the business case.

The Pallet Tax on Cube Utilization

Most ocean and truck freight is constrained by cube before it is constrained by weight. Every inch a wood pallet adds to the base of a load is an inch that cannot hold product. In a container stacked two or three loads high, 5 inches of pallet per tier compounds quickly. A corrugated slip sheet is roughly 0.03 to 0.06 inches thick — effectively eliminating that lost height.

The practical result: operations switching to PP corrugated slip sheets frequently fit an additional tier of product per container, or pack the same product into fewer containers. Even a single-tier gain across a shipping lane produces immediate, recurring freight savings.

Weight Savings and Payload Recovery

On weight-constrained lanes, the pallet tax is even starker. Replacing a 50-pound wood pallet with a sub-1-pound slip sheet returns that payload capacity to revenue product. Across a 20-pallet container, that is roughly 1,000 pounds of recovered payload — often enough to add cases without crossing axle or gross-weight limits.

Cost-Per-Load Framework

To build the case, compare total landed cost per load, not just unit price. A realistic model includes:

  • Freight per load: container or trailer cost divided by loads shipped — the number that drops when density rises.
  • Pallet cost: purchase or pooling fees, plus return logistics for reusable pools.
  • Slip sheet cost: low per-unit for one-way, amortized over trips for reusable PP sheets.
  • Handling labor: push-pull transfer time vs forklift pallet handling.

When density improvement reduces the number of containers on a lane, the freight line alone frequently covers the entire slip sheet program cost within months.

One-Way vs Reusable Economics

Corrugated plastic slip sheets support both models. For export or one-way shipments where recovery is impractical, a lighter-gauge sheet keeps per-trip cost minimal. For closed-loop domestic lanes, a heavier reusable PP sheet withstands hundreds of cycles, driving cost-per-trip toward pennies. Buyers can order either profile as custom corrugated plastic sheets sized to the load footprint and tab configuration their equipment requires.

Storage and Warehouse Density

The density advantage does not stop at the container. Empty wood pallets consume enormous warehouse footprint; a stack of slip sheets for the same number of loads occupies a fraction of the space. Storage of unused sheets is also stable — PP does not absorb moisture, warp, or harbor pests the way wood does.

Bottom Line

If your freight is cube- or weight-constrained, the pallet is costing you product on every load. Model your cost per load including freight, pallet, slip sheet, and labor, and the density math usually makes the switch obvious. Slip sheets turn wasted pallet volume back into billable payload — on every shipment, for the life of the lane.
