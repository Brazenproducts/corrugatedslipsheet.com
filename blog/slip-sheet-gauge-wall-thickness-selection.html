Slip Sheet Gauge and Wall Thickness: A Selection Guide

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • October 2026

The single most common slip sheet failure is specifying the wrong material thickness for the job. Too thin and sheets tear or curl under load; too thick and you waste money and strain the push-pull attachment. Getting gauge and wall thickness right is the foundation of a slip sheet program that performs and lasts.

Understanding Gauge in Twin-Wall PP

For PP corrugated slip sheets, thickness is expressed as overall board caliper, typically ranging from around 2mm for light duty up to 5mm or more for heavy loads. The twin-wall flute structure between the two faces gives the sheet its stiffness, so caliper alone does not tell the whole story. Flute geometry and resin density both influence how a given thickness performs under tension and bending during the pull cycle.

Match Thickness to Load Weight

Start with the heaviest load the sheet will carry. Light consumer-goods loads under 1,000 lbs are often served by thinner gauges, while palletized loads in the 2,000-plus lb range demand heavier caliper to resist tearing at the gripped lip. The lip, the tab the push-pull jaws clamp, concentrates stress, so it must be thick and stiff enough to transmit the full pull force without delaminating or stretching. Under-spec here and sheets fail at the lip first.

Lip Configuration

Sheets come in one-lip, two-lip, or four-lip configurations. A single lip suits simple pull-from-one-side operations; opposing two-lip sheets support bidirectional handling; four-lip designs enable access from any side at the cost of more material. More lips add cost and marginally more material to grip, so choose the minimum configuration that fits your dock workflow rather than defaulting to four.

Attachment and Platen Compatibility

The push-pull attachment dictates minimum lip length and sheet flatness tolerance. A platen that slides under the sheet needs a sheet that lies flat and does not curl at the edges, which is where non-absorbent PP outperforms fiber: it holds shape in humidity and cold where paper warps. Confirm your attachment's required lip dimensions before finalizing the spec, because a mismatch stalls the whole handling cycle.

Why Material Quality Matters

Two sheets of identical caliper can perform very differently depending on resin and manufacturing consistency. Virgin or high-quality FDA-compliant resin resists the micro-cracking that leads to premature lip failure. When Standard Sizes Fall Short

Off-the-shelf sheets rarely match a specific pallet footprint, product mix, and attachment exactly. Oversized sheets waste material and hang over the edge; undersized sheets leave load unsupported. A Quick Selection Checklist

Document four inputs before ordering: maximum load weight, pull direction and required lip count, attachment lip-length and flatness requirements, and handling environment including temperature and moisture. Translate load weight into a target caliper with margin, confirm the lip spec against the attachment, and verify resin quality with the supplier.

Specify Once, Run for Years

A correctly specified slip sheet quietly does its job for hundreds of cycles; a mis-specified one generates damage claims and downtime. Spend the time up front matching gauge, wall thickness, and lip configuration to real load and equipment data, and the sheet becomes one of the most reliable, lowest-cost unit-load tools in the operation.
