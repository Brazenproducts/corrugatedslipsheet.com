How to Build a Washdown and Hygiene Protocol for Reusable PP Slip Sheets

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • November 2026

Why Slip Sheet Hygiene Needs a Written Protocol

In food, beverage, and pharmaceutical operations, every surface that contacts product or shares the production environment falls under sanitation scrutiny. Reusable slip sheets cycle through receiving, storage, and shipping repeatedly, which means they can carry soil, moisture, and contamination between zones if they are not cleaned on a schedule. A written washdown protocol turns slip sheet hygiene from an afterthought into a controlled, auditable process. This how-to walks through building one step by step.

Step 1: Choose a Washable Sheet Material

Hygiene starts with material selection. Corrugated PP is well suited to washdown because polypropylene is non-absorbent, chemically resistant, and does not harbor moisture the way fiber slip sheets do. Fiber or wax-coated sheets degrade when wet and cannot be sanitized reliably. Specify PP corrugated slip sheets made from FDA-compliant resin so the sheet itself is cleanable and food-contact appropriate from the start.

Step 2: Define Cleaning Triggers

Decide in advance what events require a wash. Common triggers include:

  • Zone transition: Any sheet moving from a raw or warehouse area into a clean or packaging zone.
  • Visible soil: Any sheet with spills, debris, or residue, cleaned immediately.
  • Cycle count: A fixed number of trips after which a sheet is routed to wash regardless of appearance.
  • Allergen changeover: When a line switches to a different product with distinct allergen risk.

Documenting these triggers removes judgment calls on the floor and makes the program auditable.

Step 3: Pre-Rinse to Remove Gross Soil

Begin each wash with a pre-rinse using warm water to lift loose debris, dust, and particulate before sanitizer is applied. Removing gross soil first prevents it from shielding the surface during the sanitizing step and keeps your sanitizer working at full strength. Direct the rinse across the flutes so water does not pool inside the corrugation.

Step 4: Apply an Approved Sanitizer

Select a sanitizer compatible with polypropylene and approved for food-contact surfaces, such as a quaternary ammonium or a diluted chlorine solution within your facility's approved concentration range. Apply at the labeled dilution and observe the required contact dwell time. Polypropylene tolerates these chemistries without degrading, which is one reason PP outperforms fiber in sanitation-driven environments. Always confirm chemical compatibility and concentration against both the sanitizer label and your internal food-safety plan.

Step 5: Rinse and Dry

After the sanitizer dwell, rinse with potable water if the sanitizer requires a rinse, then dry thoroughly. Because PP is non-absorbent, sheets dry quickly, but standing water inside flutes can still cause drips in clean zones. Stand sheets on edge or use a drying rack so water drains from the corrugation. Confirm sheets are dry before they return to service.

Step 6: Inspect Before Return to Service

Every cleaned sheet should pass a quick inspection before it re-enters rotation:

  • Check for cracks, delamination, or crushed flutes that compromise load support.
  • Confirm the lip is intact and undamaged so the push-pull can grip it.
  • Verify the surface is clean, dry, and free of residue.

Pull any sheet that fails inspection out of the food-contact pool. Damaged sheets can be downgraded to non-contact or industrial use rather than discarded.

Step 7: Document and Specify for Your Facility

Finally, record wash dates, cycle counts, and inspection results so the program stands up to a food-safety audit. When you order, specify sheet dimensions, lip configuration, and gauge that match your handling equipment and load, and confirm the resin documentation for your compliance file.
