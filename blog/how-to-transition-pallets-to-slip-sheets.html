How to Transition From Pallets to Corrugated Slip Sheets

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • October 2026

Why Operations Move to Slip Sheets

Logistics and warehouse teams adopt corrugated slip sheets to recover cube space, cut tare weight, and eliminate the cost and hygiene problems of wood pallets. A slip sheet is a thin load-carrying platform that replaces the pallet beneath a unit load, handled by a push-pull attachment on a forklift. Switching from pallets to slip sheets can add several hundred pounds of payload per container and remove the pest-control and splinter concerns that come with wood.

The transition is straightforward when planned in stages. This guide walks through equipment readiness, specification, pilot testing, and full rollout so your team captures the savings without disrupting throughput.

Step 1: Confirm Equipment Compatibility

Slip-sheet handling requires a push-pull attachment mounted to a forklift in place of, or in addition to, standard forks. The attachment grips the sheet lip, pulls the load onto a platen, and pushes it off at the destination. Before committing, audit how many trucks need attachments and whether receiving partners can handle slip-sheeted loads. If a trading partner cannot, you can ship on slip sheets and re-palletize at a transfer point, or supply them with the equipment specification.

Step 2: Specify the Right Sheet

Sheet performance depends on material, thickness, and lip design. PP corrugated sheets resist moisture, tear, and repeated handling far better than solid fiber, making them ideal for reusable closed loops. Key specifications include overall dimensions, number of lips (one lip for push-pull, four for full support), and gauge matched to load weight.

  • Lip configuration matched to your push-pull attachment
  • Thickness and flute profile rated for the load weight
  • Dimensions sized to the load footprint with minimal overhang
  • Moisture resistance for cold-chain or washdown environments

Elipacko manufactures PP corrugated slip sheets in custom sizes from FDA-compliant resin, so you can match sheet dimensions and gauge to your exact unit load rather than forcing products onto a standard size.

Step 3: Run a Controlled Pilot

Select one or two high-volume lanes for the pilot. Measure load stability, handling time, damage rates, and operator feedback over several weeks. Train forklift operators on push-pull technique, since proper lip engagement is the single biggest factor in smooth handling. Document the cube and weight gains per load so you can quantify the business case before scaling.

During the pilot, test any ancillary materials your loads need, such as layer separation. Many operations pair slip sheets with reusable custom corrugated plastic sheets as tier dividers to stabilize mixed or fragile loads during transit.

Step 4: Scale and Standardize

Once the pilot proves out, expand lane by lane, prioritizing routes with the highest shipping volume and the most favorable receiving partners. Standardize on a small number of sheet sizes to simplify inventory and operator handling. Build return logistics into the plan so reusable sheets circulate back through your network, which is where the cost-per-trip advantage over disposable alternatives is realized.
