Slip Sheets vs. Pallets: ROI Analysis for Pallet-Free Warehouse Operations

Corrugatedslipsheet Editorial • September 2026

Questioning the Pallet: When Slip Sheets Win on ROI

The pallet is so deeply embedded in global logistics infrastructure that questioning it feels counterintuitive. But for operations evaluating material handling costs at scale — particularly in container-loaded imports, cold-chain distribution, and food-manufacturing environments — the slip sheet alternative deserves a serious financial review. PP corrugated slip sheets eliminate pallet cost, reduce storage cube requirements, and lower inbound freight weight — savings that compound significantly across high-volume distribution operations.

The Real Cost of Pallet-Based Handling

Wood pallets are often treated as a low-cost commodity, but the total cost of ownership in a managed pallet program is substantially higher than the purchase price suggests:

  • Purchase or rental cost: New GMA wood pallets run $12–$22 each; heat-treated export pallets are higher. Pool rental (CHEP, iGPS) typically costs $3–$7 per trip plus repair and loss fees.
  • Repair and replacement: Wood pallets break, accumulate ISPM 15 compliance complexity, and generate maintenance costs of $1–$4 per pallet per year in managed programs.
  • Storage cube: Empty pallets consume 150mm of vertical stack height. A 1,000-pallet storage buffer represents significant floor space and cube cost in high-value distribution centers.
  • Freight weight: A standard GMA wood pallet weighs 17–27kg. At 26 pallets per trailer, that's 440–700kg of non-revenue payload per load — a meaningful freight cost that compounds across a high-volume fleet.
  • Return logistics: Empty pallet return is a dedicated logistics cost in single-direction supply chains. In open distributions, it's often a net loss.

What PP Corrugated Slip Sheets Actually Cost

High-quality PP corrugated slip sheets from Elipacko cost approximately $2.50 to $5.50 per unit at factory-direct pricing, depending on thickness (3mm to 5mm), footprint size, and volume. For multi-use programs, the per-trip cost is even lower:

  • $3.50 slip sheet ÷ 15 uses = $0.23 per trip
  • $4.50 slip sheet ÷ 20 uses = $0.23 per trip
  • Single-use comparison: $3.50 per trip vs. wood pallet rental at $4–$7 per trip

On a cost-per-trip basis, PP corrugated slip sheets compete directly with wood pallets even at single use — and beat them handily in any multi-use scenario. For single-direction ocean container shipments, slip sheets at single-use pricing often cost less than one-way wood pallet programs while improving container cube utilization.

Equipment Investment: Push-Pull Forklift Attachments

The primary capital requirement for slip sheet handling is a push-pull forklift attachment — a hydraulic platen that extends to receive the slip sheet load and pulls it onto the fork tines. Push-pull attachments cost $5,000 to $15,000 per forklift depending on capacity rating and manufacturer. For operations with existing forklift fleets, attachment retrofit is typically feasible.

ROI on push-pull equipment is volume-dependent. At 200 loads per day across 250 operating days — saving $3.50 per load versus wood pallet rental — annual savings are $175,000. A $50,000 investment in push-pull attachments pays back in under four months at that rate.

Where Slip Sheets Deliver the Strongest ROI

Slip sheet handling delivers measurably stronger returns in specific operational contexts:

  • Container-loaded imports/exports: Slip sheets eliminate pallet weight and cube in ocean containers, significantly increasing load efficiency. A 40-foot container can typically fit 25–30% more product without pallets.
  • Retailer DC operations: Where product moves through a single DC before store delivery, push-pull handling is efficient and pallet return logistics are eliminated entirely.
  • Cold storage facilities: PP corrugated slip sheets outperform wood in cold and frozen environments — no moisture absorption, no hygiene risk, no phytosanitary complexity.
  • Food and pharmaceutical manufacturing: Slip sheets eliminate the contamination risk associated with wood pallets in FSMA and cGMP facility environments.

Building Your Slip Sheet ROI Model

A straightforward ROI model for slip sheet conversion uses these inputs: (annual pallet cost − annual slip sheet cost) − push-pull equipment amortization − operational changeover cost = net annual savings. To model conservatively, assume 10% higher slip sheet attrition and 15% lower container utilization gain than optimistic projections. Most operations with 50 or more loads per day see positive ROI within 12–24 months of program implementation — and significantly faster in container-heavy import operations where cube utilization gains are immediate and measurable.
